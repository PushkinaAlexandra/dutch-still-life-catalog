Dutch Golden Age: Still Life Collection

12 Masterpieces from the Metropolitan Museum of Art · curated by Alexandra Pushkina

Still Life with Lobster and Fruit

Abraham van Beyeren

probably early 1650s

A luxurious still life with a lobster, exotic fruits, and a silver tazza. The vibrant palette and virtuoso rendering of textures reflect the wealth and status of the Dutch Golden Age. The painting showcases mastery in depicting light on metal, glass, and the velvety skin of fruits.

Pronkstilleven

Still Life with a Skull and a Writing Quill

Pieter Claesz

1628

A classic example of the "vanitas" genre — a meditation on the transience of life. The skull, extinguished candle, and quill symbolize the futility of earthly labors in the face of death. The restrained monochrome palette reinforces the philosophical mood.

Vanitas

Vanitas Still Life

Edwaert Collier

1662

A theatrical vanitas composition: a skull, musical instruments, a globe, and books. Each object reminds us of the vanity of worldly pleasures and the inevitability of death. The play of light and shadow adds dramatic tension to the scene.

Vanitas

Vanitas Still Life

Jacques de Gheyn II

1603

One of the earliest vanitas still lifes in European art. The skull, flowers, and soap bubble symbolize the fragility of life and the fleeting nature of beauty. The work is distinguished by fine detail and rich symbolism.

Vanitas

A Vase of Flowers

Margareta Haverman

1716

A rare work by a 18th-century female artist. An elegant bouquet in a glass vase is painted with astonishing precision: every petal and leaf is rendered with botanical care. The bright, joyful palette distinguishes this piece from darker vanitas scenes.

Flower Still Life

Still Life with a Glass and Oysters

Jan Davidsz de Heem

ca. 1640

An exquisite small-scale still life: oysters, a wine glass, and a lemon. The rendering of glass transparency and the wet gleam of oysters is a testament to virtuoso technique. The work reflects the Dutch love for refined table details.

Banketje / Ontbijtje

Still Life: A Banqueting Scene

Jan Davidsz de Heem

probably ca. 1640–41

A monumental banquet painting. On the table are fruits, a lobster, silverware, lemons, and luxurious fabrics. The composition conveys the abundance and opulence of a Dutch aristocratic feast. Every detail is rendered with jewelry-like precision.

Pronkstilleven

Still Life with Fruit, Glassware, and a Wanli Bowl

Willem Kalf

1659

Kalf's mastery in rendering light and texture reaches its peak here. A Chinese Wanli porcelain vase, glassware, and fruits are painted with near-photographic precision. The play of reflections on polished surfaces is mesmerizing.

Pronkstilleven

Still Life with Poppy, Insects, and Reptiles

Otto Marseus van Schrieck

ca. 1670

An unusual forest-floor still life: poppy, insects, and reptiles. Marseus van Schrieck worked in the "bosstilleven" genre, blending botanical precision with elements of grotesque and surrealism. The dark background highlights the vividness of the flowers and the detailed creatures.

Sottobosco

A Vase with Flowers

Jacob Vosmaer

probably 1613

An early Dutch flower still life. A large vase with a lush bouquet of peonies, irises, and roses. The work is distinguished by its decorative richness and delicate color harmony. One of the earliest examples of this genre in the Met collection.

Gamepiece with a Dead Heron

Jan Weenix

1695

An impressive hunting still life: a dead heron, game birds, and fruits set against a park landscape. The painting combines still life with landscape, reflecting the influence of aristocratic hunting culture. Dramatic lighting and sculptural composition create a vivid sense of presence.

Hunting / Gamepiece

Still Life with Oysters, a Silver Tazza, and Glassware

Willem Claesz Heda

1635

A masterpiece of the Dutch "breakfast" piece (banketje). Oysters, a silver tazza, an overturned glass, and a lemon are rendered in a restrained silvery-green palette. Heda masterfully conveys the textures of metal, glass, and the moist rind of the lemon — a classic of monochrome still life.
